Universal principles · Data

Records you own, made readable

Data is the raw material of every answer. The goal is not to collect more of it, but to own what you have and make it readable, so that a question gets an answer instead of a search session.

What it is

Data here means the records of your own life or your own organisation: photos, health measurements, books read, trips taken, invoices, agreements. Owned means they live in a place and a format you control. Readable means a script, a query or an AI can open them without asking a vendor first.

Why it matters

Most services let you look at your data but not ask questions of it. A wearable shows a graph of your sleep; it will not tell you how your sleep relates to your travel. Once the records are yours and readable, those questions become a query.

It also changes what an archive is. A folder of exports is dead weight until something reads it. The moment it can be queried, it becomes a source.

What it looks like

At home. Health data from my watch, my reading history and 271 books, my photo library: each lands as files I own, and each is loaded into a local database that I can query and join with the others.

At work. The same principle is data ownership in a platform: the organisation keeps its records in its own warehouse, not only inside the tools that created them, so it can combine them across systems.

The usual mistake

Collecting without reading. Saving everything feels productive, but an unread archive answers nothing. Pick the questions you want answered, and make the data for those questions readable first.
